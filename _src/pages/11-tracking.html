Getting the data in

Links, coupons, and a sale that never had a click

Four ways in, so the way your business actually works is one of them.

Tracking links

Every partner gets links. A click is claimed first-party by a two-line JavaScript snippet on your own site.

Coupon codes

A coupon attributes a sale even when no click ever happened — the podcast mention, the printed card, the Discord message nobody clicked through.

Server events

Report sales straight from your backend over a REST API with API keys, whatever your front end is doing.

Stripe, with no JavaScript at all

Stripe sales can attribute through Stripe’s own client_reference_id, so a merchant running no tracking script can still credit partners.

Precisely: publishable keys can identify a customer but can never report revenue. Secret keys live server-side. The key that can move a number is not the key in your page source.

The rules

Last-touch, with a window you set

Attribution is last-touch across a window that defaults to 30 days and is configured per programme, not per account. A 7-day window for the impulse product and a 90-day one for the enterprise plan can live side by side.

Where two touches compete, sources are ranked: API, then link, then direct, then coupon, then import.

Precisely: every decision is stored with its full reasoning — every touch considered, which were eligible, which won, which lost and why, and a confidence score. The debugger reads that record; it does not recompute one.

Nobody won

Sales attributed to nobody are recorded as exactly that

Most of your revenue probably has no partner behind it, and that is fine. What is not fine is a system that silently drops those sales, because then no total you produce can ever be checked against your own books.

Refficks writes the decision down either way, with the reason.

Precisely: the debugger works on unattributed sales too. “Nobody had a claim” is a stored decision with reasoning, not an absence of data.

The guarantees

What tracking refuses to do

Refuses to

Store your visitors’ IP addresses

Refficks never stores a raw IP address. Fraud signals run on salted hashes, and Do Not Track is respected by default.

Refuses to

Process the same event twice

Duplicates are structurally impossible: idempotency is enforced by the database. A retried webhook or a double-submitted event resolves to the original.

Refuses to

Change an attribution quietly

Attribution is never silently altered. A correction supersedes the earlier decision and both remain readable, with the reasoning that produced each.
