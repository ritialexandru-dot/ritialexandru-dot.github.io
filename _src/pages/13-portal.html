Automations

They fire on real events and do exactly three things

Partner approved. First sale. Gone dormant. Automations run off things that actually happened, not a schedule you have to keep in your head.

And they may do three things: send an email, add a tag, enrol in a campaign. An automation cannot touch money. Ever.

Precisely: this is a bounded action set, not a permission you can widen in settings. There is no branch of the code where an automation writes to the ledger.

Email

Eight emails that work on day one

You should not have to write a welcome email before your first partner can be approved. Refficks ships eight defaults, and you can override any of them.

Your copy is text, not code

Merchant-authored templates are substituted, never compiled. Copy typed into a form is never handed to a template engine.

Precisely: a stray brace in your email copy cannot execute anything, because nothing in that field is ever evaluated.

Every send is recorded

“Did they get the email?” has an answer, with a date on it, whichever of the eight it was.

Precisely: the send log is part of the partner’s record, so the answer survives staff turnover.

Unsubscribing takes one click

A partner can turn programme email off with one click and no sign-in — and still receive emails about their own money.

Precisely: opting out of marketing does not opt anybody out of being told they have been paid.

Their record, their responsibility

Partners maintain their own details

Contact details and payout details belong to the partner, and they change them themselves. That is one less inbox request for you, and one less person handling somebody’s bank details.

You see that the record changed and when. You do not see the bank details, because nobody does.

Precisely: payout details are encrypted at rest and write-only — never displayed back to anyone, including the partner who typed them.

Refuses to

Show anyone a bank account

Write-only means write-only. If a partner forgets what they entered, they enter it again. That is the trade, and it is the right one.

Refuses to

Hide money behind a preference

Turning off programme email never turns off the email that says a payout was sent.
