Plans

Percentage, fixed, or tiered. One-time or recurring.

A plan can pay a percentage, a flat amount, or a tier that changes with volume. It can pay once or on every renewal, with an optional duration — twelve months of recurring commission, then it stops.

Plans carry conditions and priorities, so a launch rate and a standard rate can coexist without anyone maintaining a spreadsheet of exceptions. And when you do want a deliberate exception, per-partner custom plans exist for exactly that.

Precisely: money is integer minor units plus an ISO currency, everywhere. Never floats, never a silent conversion between currencies.

Payouts

You see who cannot be paid before you commit

The preview shows who is owed, who cannot be paid, and exactly why — no payout details, below the minimum — while there is still time to do something about it.

Approve it and the batch is built in one transaction and exported as a CSV your bank can use. A person approves it. A person marks it paid, with a reference.

Precisely: partners’ payout details are encrypted at rest and write-only. They are never displayed back to anyone, including the partner who typed them.

Reporting

The dashboard and the ledger are the same arithmetic

Revenue, commission, clicks and conversions over the period you pick, per-partner league tables, and day-by-day charts that include the days nothing happened.

No confident zeroes

An empty programme says so

Lesser software prints $0.00 for a programme with no data and +100% for a first month with nothing to compare against. Both are lies with a decimal point in them.

Precisely: closed days come from nightly rollups and today is computed live from the same arithmetic, so no total silently changes when a day rolls over. Days close in your organization’s timezone, not the server’s.

Approval

Commission is approved on purpose

Entries do not become payable because time passed. A merchant approves them, one at a time or in bulk, and the partner sees the same arithmetic for their own earnings that you see for your liability.

There is no version of the numbers that only one side can read.

Precisely: the partner portal renders the same stored arithmetic — “20% of USD 299.00” — not a separately computed figure that might drift from yours.

Refuses to

Move your money

Refficks never moves money itself. It builds the batch and hands you a file. There is always a step where a person can say no.

Refuses to

Show a bank account back

Payout details are write-only. A merchant sees that the record changed, never what it changed to.

Refuses to

Let an automation pay anyone

An automation may send an email, add a tag, or enrol a partner in a campaign. It cannot touch money. Ever.
