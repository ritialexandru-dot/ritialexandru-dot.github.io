How the importer behaves

Read it back first. Then decide.

Your file, unedited

A CSV or a LeadDyno affiliates export, as downloaded. Column names are recognised as they come, so nobody has to open the spreadsheet and start renaming headers before the tool will look at it.

Nothing happens until you say so

The file is read back before anything is written: how many rows there are, how many can be imported, and which lines cannot and why. The import button comes after the report, not before it.

One bad row never loses the file

A malformed line is named and skipped. Fix it and re-import — re-importing never duplicates anyone, and never overwrites an existing record unless you explicitly ask it to.

Imported partners are ordinary partners

Same audit trail, same welcome automation, same everything. There is no second-class record with features missing because of how it arrived.

Precisely: matching is by email, and a collision is reported as a collision rather than resolved by guessing. The failed-row report is a file you can fix and feed straight back in.

The honest part

Your old commission totals do not become Refficks commission

This is the trade, and it is the part most migration pages bury. Read it before you start, not after.

Historical commission totals from another platform come across as that platform’s reported figures, labelled as such on the partner. They are context. They are not entries in your ledger.

The reason is the whole product. The Refficks ledger holds money it can explain — every entry stores the arithmetic that produced it and the attribution decision behind that. A number imported from elsewhere has none of that. Writing it into the ledger would mean the ledger contains figures it cannot justify, and then no total in the system is trustworthy, including the ones it computed itself.

So your ledger starts clean, on the day you start. Your partners keep their history as visible context beside it.

Precisely: imported totals are stored on the partner record with their source and the date they were reported. They are never summed into a Refficks balance, a payout preview, or a partner’s earnings.

What you keep

  • Every partner, with their contact details and their status.
  • Their coupon codes and their tracking links.
  • Their historical totals, labelled and visible on their profile.
  • The relationship. Nobody has to sign up again.

What you do not

  • Historical commission as ledger entries you can pay out from.
  • Historical clicks and conversions as Refficks events.
  • Attribution decisions from the old platform, which were made under rules Refficks cannot inspect.
Refuses to

Make you clean the file first

The export as downloaded is the input. If a column is called Affiliate Email, that is fine.

Refuses to

Import before you have looked

You see the counts and the failures first. The button is on the far side of the report.

Refuses to

Put unexplainable money in the ledger

A ledger that only holds money it can explain is the product. Imported totals stay labelled beside it.
